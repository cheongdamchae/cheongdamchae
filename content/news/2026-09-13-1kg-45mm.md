    Naengsam is meant to be eaten as it comes off the iron. That does not mean
    rushing it. Flip it too fast and you lose what makes this cut interesting.


    It comes down to the 4.5mm. Leave a slice a little longer and the edges go
    crisp. Pull it early and the inside stays juicy. Same meat, two ways to eat
    it, and you get to choose on the day.


    ### Why 4.5mm


    Cut it thinner and there is no choice to make — it cooks the moment it
    lands, and crisp is all you get. Cut it thicker and you lose the lightness
    that makes naengsam what it is.


    We use Duroc belly, and we changed the thickness more than once before we
    stopped. 4.5mm is where both are still possible. If our naengsam tastes
    different from the last one you had, this is why.


    ### No foil on the plate


    The pork goes straight onto cast iron. Foil traps the melted fat and the
    meat ends up steamed. Metal contact is what gives you the crisp edge.


    Lay the garlic on the iron beside the meat — it is worth it. Ssamjang,
    lettuce, salt and sesame oil: just ask and we will bring more.


    ### Side dishes, made every morning


    Nine of them on this day. They are made fresh in the kitchen each morning,
    so the line-up shifts a little. What goes out is decided that morning.
